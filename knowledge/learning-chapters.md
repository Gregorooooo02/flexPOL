## Chapter 1 — Why Invest?

*Objective: the learner can explain why investing matters and match a goal to a time horizon and a level of risk.*

| # | Subchapter | Key ideas | Quiz / interaction idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Why investing is worth it** | Inflation slowly eats the value of cash; compounding grows money over time; saving and investing are different things | Slider: "100 in cash vs. invested, after 10 / 20 / 30 years" |
| 1.2 | **Setting goals** | Short-, medium- and long-term goals; specific vs. vague goals; why the goal comes before the instrument | Sort goals into horizons: a holiday, a home deposit, a pension |
| 1.3 | **Time horizon and liquidity** | How long until you need the money, and whether you can get it out early; an emergency fund comes first | "Maya needs money in 8 months. Which instrument fits badly, and why?" |
| 1.4 | **Risk and volatility** | Volatility is price swings, not only loss; risk vs. expected return; risk tolerance vs. risk capacity | Match 3 price charts to low, medium and high volatility |
| 1.5 | **Diversification** | Don't put all your eggs in one basket; concentration risk; diversification reduces risk but doesn't remove it | Same market shock, two portfolios: which survives better? |

**Mission: "The Two-Year Goal"** *(the MVP mission)*. The learner allocates fictional money across the sandbox instruments. Then an event hits: the volatile asset drops and an unexpected bill arrives. The AI debriefs, the learner retries, gets a score, and **Free Play unlocks**.

> Liquidity is covered in 1.3. The MVP learning objective mentions liquidity needs explicitly, and the Locked-term instrument only makes sense if the learner understands it.

## Chapter 2 — Know Your Instruments

*Objective: the learner can describe the main instrument types by risk, return, liquidity and cost, and pick the right tool for a goal.*

| # | Subchapter | Key ideas | Sandbox link |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Cash and savings accounts** | Safe and accessible, but loses value to inflation | Accessible Cash |
| 2.2 | **Deposits and bonds** | Lending money for interest; fixed term; penalties for early withdrawal; credit risk | Locked-term instrument |
| 2.3 | **Stocks** | Owning a share of a company; dividends and growth; high volatility | Volatile concentrated asset |
| 2.4 | **Funds and ETFs** | Instant diversification; index vs. actively managed funds | Diversified market basket |
| 2.5 | **Costs and fees** | Management fees and transaction costs; how small fees add up over decades | Fee comparison of two fictional baskets |
| 2.6 | **High-risk and speculative assets** | Crypto, leverage, "hot tips"; why hype is not a strategy | Fictional "trending" asset with a hype event |
| 2.7 | **Comparing instruments** | Comparison table: risk, return, liquidity, cost, horizon | Interactive "build the table" quiz |

**Mission: "Pick the Right Tool"**. The learner gets three fictional goals with different horizons and assigns each one an instrument mix. The coach challenges any mismatch, for example a locked-term instrument for an emergency fund. **Quest Arena unlocks.**

## Chapter 3 — Investing Through Life

*Objective: the learner can adapt a plan to their life stage and explain why retirement investing starts early.*

| # | Subchapter | Key ideas | Quiz / interaction idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Your life stage changes your plan** | Horizon, income, responsibilities and risk capacity all shift with age | Order the life-stage cards by typical risk capacity |
| 3.2 | **Starting out (18–30)** | First salary, emergency fund, small regular contributions, the power of starting early | "Start at 25 vs. 35" compounding comparison |
| 3.3 | **Building up (30–50)** | Family, a home, juggling several goals at once, keeping contributions going | Split a budget across 3 competing goals |
| 3.4 | **Approaching retirement (50+)** | Reducing risk over time (glide path); protecting what you've built; sequence risk | Same crash at age 30 vs. age 62: which hurts more? |
| 3.5 | **Why invest for a pension** | Public pensions may not be enough; the retirement gap; you're the one responsible for the long term | Estimate a fictional retirement gap |
| 3.6 | **Pension vehicles** | Employer schemes, personal retirement accounts, tax advantages, lock-up rules | Match each account type to its features |
| 3.7 | **Staying on course** | Rebalancing, reviewing your plan every year, avoiding panic-selling | A drifted portfolio: rebalance it |

**Mission: "One Life, Four Decisions"**. The learner follows one fictional character across four life stages and adjusts the allocation at each one, with an event at each stage. The score rewards adapting the plan, not only the final balance.

> 3.6 can stay generic, or be localized to a specific country's pension system later. If it is localized, keep the content educational and avoid anything that reads as a product recommendation, to match the AI coach's boundary.

## Capstone (optional) — Ready for the Real World?

A short wrap-up. The learner reviews their sandbox decisions, then builds a checklist of what to verify before investing real money, and what they still need to learn. This meets the success criterion "identify what they still need to understand."
