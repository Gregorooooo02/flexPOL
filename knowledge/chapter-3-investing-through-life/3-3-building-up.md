# 3.3 Building Up (30–50)

**Chapter:** 3 — Investing Through Life
**Goal of this subchapter:** the learner can balance several goals with different horizons and keep long-term investing going while life gets busier.

---

## Lesson

### Many goals at once

Mid-life often brings several financial goals at the same time:

- a home or a larger home,
- children and their education,
- a car, travel, caring for parents,
- and still: **retirement**.

Each goal has its **own horizon**, so each can have its **own allocation**.

### One person, three "buckets"

> **Sam, 38**, earns a steady income and wants to:
>
> | Goal | Horizon | Possible allocation |
> |---|---|---|
> | Emergency fund | Any time | 100% cash |
> | Home renovation | 3 years | Mostly deposits and bonds, little or no stocks |
> | Retirement | 27 years | Mostly a broad market basket |
>
> Thinking in buckets stops short-term goals from taking too much risk, and long-term goals from taking too little.

### Protecting the plan

With more people depending on you, protection matters more:

- **Emergency fund:** often 3–6 months of essential expenses, or more if income is unstable.
- **Insurance:** life, health and income protection can stop one event from ruining the whole plan.
- **Avoid lifestyle inflation:** when income rises, raise savings too, not only spending.

### Don't pause retirement

A common mistake is stopping retirement contributions "for a few years" to fund other goals. Those years are valuable compounding time. Even a smaller monthly amount is better than stopping completely.

### Key takeaways

- Mid-life often means juggling several goals at once.
- Give each goal its own horizon and allocation.
- Protect the plan with an emergency fund and insurance.
- Keep retirement contributions going, even if smaller.

## Control questions

### Q1. Why should different goals have different allocations?
- A) To make the plan look complicated
- B) Because the law requires it
- C) Because each goal has its own horizon and risk capacity ✅
- D) They shouldn't; one allocation fits everything.

**Explanation:** money needed in 3 years and money needed in 27 years face different risks.

### Q2. Sam needs money for a renovation in 3 years. Which allocation fits best?
- A) Mostly deposits and bonds, with little or no stocks ✅
- B) 100% in one volatile stock
- C) 100% in a 10-year locked instrument
- D) Speculative assets

**Explanation:** a short-to-medium horizon needs stability and access on time.

### Q3. What is "lifestyle inflation"?
- A) The cost of insurance
- B) A type of investment fund
- C) Rising prices in shops
- D) Spending more every time income rises, so savings don't grow ✅

**Explanation:** if spending grows as fast as income, there's nothing extra to invest.

### Q4. Money is tight because of a new child. What's usually better for retirement savings?
- A) Stop all contributions for 10 years.
- B) Lower contributions for a while, but keep them going. ✅
- C) Move everything into a speculative asset to catch up.
- D) Sell all investments.

**Explanation:** keeping even a small amount invested preserves compounding time and the habit.
