# 3.4 Approaching Retirement (50+)

**Chapter:** 3 — Investing Through Life
**Goal of this subchapter:** the learner understands why risk is usually reduced near retirement, what a glide path is, and what sequence risk means.

---

## Lesson

### From growing to protecting

For decades, the main job of retirement savings is to **grow**. As retirement gets close, the job gradually shifts to **protecting** what's been built, and later to **providing income**.

### The glide path

A **glide path** is a plan that slowly shifts an allocation from growth-oriented to steadier as the target date approaches.

| Age | Market basket | Bonds / deposits | Cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 85% | 10% | 5% |
| 45 | 70% | 25% | 5% |
| 55 | 55% | 38% | 7% |
| 65 | 40% | 50% | 10% |

*Illustrative example only. Real plans depend on the person.*

Note that even at 65, part of the money stays invested for growth. Retirement can last 20–30 years, and that money still needs to keep up with inflation.

### Sequence risk: when a crash hits matters

**Sequence risk** is the danger that markets fall **right before or early in retirement**, just when you start withdrawing money.

> **Crash at age 30:** savings are still small, contributions continue, there are decades to recover, and new contributions buy at lower prices.
>
> **Crash at age 64:** savings are at their largest, withdrawals are about to start, and selling at low prices locks in the losses.

The same percentage drop can have a much bigger effect late in the journey.

### Ways to reduce sequence risk

- Gradually **lower risk** in the years before retirement (the glide path).
- Keep **1–3 years of planned spending** in cash or short-term deposits, so you don't have to sell investments during a crash.
- Stay **flexible**: spend a bit less in bad years if possible.

### Key takeaways

- Near retirement, the focus shifts from growth to protection.
- A glide path lowers risk gradually, not all at once.
- Sequence risk means a crash near retirement hurts the most.
- A cash buffer helps avoid selling at the worst moment.

## Control questions

### Q1. What is a glide path?
- A) A sudden switch from stocks to cash at retirement
- B) A gradual shift from growth-oriented to steadier investments as a target date approaches ✅
- C) A type of pension
- D) A fee schedule

**Explanation:** it lowers risk step by step over many years.

### Q2. A 30% crash hits at age 30 and again at age 64. When does it usually hurt the plan more?
- A) At 30
- B) At 64 ✅
- C) Equally at both ages
- D) It never matters.

**Explanation:** at 64 the savings are larger, withdrawals are near, and there's little time to recover.

### Q3. Why does part of the money stay invested in the market basket even at 65?
- A) By mistake
- B) Retirement can last decades, and the money still needs to grow and beat inflation. ✅
- C) It can't be moved.
- D) Bonds are not allowed after 65.

**Explanation:** a retirement of 20–30 years is still a long horizon for part of the money.

### Q4. How does a cash buffer of 1–3 years of spending help in retirement?
- A) It earns the highest return.
- B) It lets you avoid selling investments during a market drop. ✅
- C) It removes all risk.
- D) It replaces a pension.

**Explanation:** spending comes from the buffer while investments recover.

### Q5. What is sequence risk?
- A) The risk of choosing funds in the wrong order
- B) The risk that poor returns happen right before or early in retirement ✅
- C) The risk of paying fees in sequence
- D) The risk of inflation over many years

**Explanation:** the timing of losses, not only their size, shapes the outcome.

### Q6 (reflection). Why is it a bad idea to move everything to cash on the first day of retirement?

**What a good answer mentions:** inflation slowly eats cash over a long retirement, and part of the money still has a long horizon.
