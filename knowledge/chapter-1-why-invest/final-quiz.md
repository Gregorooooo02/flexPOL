# Chapter 1 — Final Quiz: Why Invest?

**Covers:** 1.1 Why investing is worth it · 1.2 Setting goals · 1.3 Time horizon and liquidity · 1.4 Risk and volatility · 1.5 Diversification
**Questions:** 10 · **Suggested pass mark:** 7/10

> All numbers are fictional and simplified.

---

### Q1. Prices rise 4% a year, and your savings account pays 1%. What happens to your money over time?
- A) It's fully protected from inflation.
- B) It grows in real terms.
- C) Its purchasing power slowly shrinks. ✅
- D) It stays exactly the same in value.

**Explanation:** interest below inflation means each year the money buys a little less. *(1.1)*

### Q2. Which effect makes money grow faster the longer it stays invested?
- A) Compounding ✅
- B) Diversification
- C) Liquidity
- D) Volatility

**Explanation:** returns earn their own returns, so growth speeds up with time. *(1.1)*

### Q3. Which of these is a clear financial goal?
- A) "I want to invest smarter."
- B) "I want to have more money than my friends."
- C) "I want to save something every now and then."
- D) "I want 3,000 coins for a new laptop in 10 months." ✅

**Explanation:** a clear goal names what, how much and when. *(1.2)*

### Q4. Jan wants to save for retirement in 35 years, a car in 4 years and a holiday in 6 months. Which list gives their horizons in the same order as the goals above?
- A) Long, short, short
- B) Long, medium, short ✅
- C) Medium, medium, long
- D) Short, medium, long

**Explanation:** over 10 years is long, 2–10 years is medium, under 2 years is short. *(1.2)*

### Q5. What should usually be in place before you start investing for long-term goals?
- A) A single stock you believe in
- B) A leveraged product
- C) A 10-year locked-term instrument
- D) An emergency fund ✅

**Explanation:** an emergency fund prevents forced selling or penalties when surprises happen. *(1.2, 1.3)*

### Q6. Lena puts her whole emergency fund into a 3-year Locked-term instrument because it pays more than cash. What's the main problem?
- A) She may not be able to get the money when an emergency happens. ✅
- B) Locked-term instruments are very volatile.
- C) The return is too high.
- D) There is no problem.

**Explanation:** an emergency fund needs high liquidity; locking it defeats its purpose. *(1.3)*

### Q7. Which statement about volatility is correct?
- A) Cash is the most volatile instrument.
- B) A volatile investment always ends in a loss.
- C) Volatility describes how much a price swings up and down. ✅
- D) Volatility only matters for long-term goals.

**Explanation:** volatility is about swings in both directions, not a guaranteed loss. *(1.4)*

### Q8. Your investment of 2,000 coins falls to 1,000. How much must it now rise to get back to 2,000?
- A) 50%
- B) 75%
- C) 100% ✅
- D) 200%

**Explanation:** 1,000 has to double, which is a 100% gain. *(1.4)*

### Q9. Kai feels relaxed about big price swings, but needs his money in 9 months for university fees. How much risk should his plan take?
- A) Low risk, because his short horizon limits his risk capacity ✅
- B) It doesn't matter.
- C) Only speculative assets
- D) High risk, because he's relaxed about swings

**Explanation:** risk capacity (what you can afford) should guide the plan, not only risk tolerance. *(1.4)*

### Q10. Portfolio A is 100% in one company. Portfolio B is spread across a broad market basket, some bonds and some cash. The single company drops 70%. Which statement is true?
- A) Portfolio B can never lose money.
- B) Both portfolios fall 70%.
- C) Portfolio A is safer because it's simpler.
- D) Portfolio B falls much less, because no single company decides its outcome. ✅

**Explanation:** diversification limits the damage from one bad event, though it can't remove all risk. *(1.5)*
