# 2.6 High-Risk and Speculative Assets

**Chapter:** 2 — Know Your Instruments
**Sandbox link:** fictional "trending" asset with a hype event
**Goal of this subchapter:** the learner can recognize speculative assets and hype-driven decisions, and understands why they should stay a small part of a plan, if any.

---

## Lesson

### Investing vs. speculating

- **Investing:** putting money into assets that are expected to produce value over time, like company profits or interest.
- **Speculating:** buying mainly because you hope the **price** will rise and someone will pay more later, often without any underlying income.

Speculation isn't always wrong, but it is closer to a bet than a plan.

### Examples of high-risk assets

| Asset | Why it's risky |
|---|---|
| **Cryptocurrencies** | Extreme volatility, no underlying income, prices driven largely by sentiment |
| **Leveraged products** | You invest with borrowed money, so gains **and losses** are multiplied; you can lose more than you put in |
| **Small or unknown companies** ("penny stocks") | Little information, easy to manipulate, can collapse to zero |
| **"Hot tips"** from social media | Often late, biased or part of a scheme |

### The hype cycle

1. A price starts rising, and stories spread.
2. More people buy because others are buying (**fear of missing out**, or FOMO).
3. The price goes far above any reasonable value.
4. Early buyers sell, the mood turns, and the price crashes.
5. Late buyers are left with large losses.

### Warning signs

- "Guaranteed" or "risk-free" high returns
- Pressure to "act now before it's too late"
- Returns that rely only on new buyers joining
- No clear explanation of how the asset produces value
- Influencers paid to promote it

### If you want to speculate anyway

- Only use money you can **afford to lose completely**.
- Keep it a **small part** of your portfolio.
- Never use your **emergency fund** or money for near-term goals.
- Never borrow to speculate.

### Key takeaways

- Speculation is a bet on price, not a plan built on value.
- Leverage multiplies losses as well as gains.
- Hype and FOMO lead people to buy high and sell low.
- Keep speculative bets small and separate from important goals.

## Control questions

### Q1. What is the main difference between investing and speculating?
- A) There is none.
- B) Investing relies on value produced over time; speculating relies mostly on price going up. ✅
- C) Speculating is always illegal.
- D) Investing is only for professionals.

**Explanation:** speculation is a bet on price, usually without underlying income.

### Q2. What does leverage do?
- A) It lowers your risk.
- B) It multiplies both gains and losses. ✅
- C) It guarantees a profit.
- D) It removes fees.

**Explanation:** borrowed money makes every move bigger, in both directions.

### Q3. A social media post says: "This coin will 10x next week, buy NOW!" Which is the best reaction?
- A) Buy immediately before you miss out.
- B) Put your emergency fund into it.
- C) Treat it as a warning sign and don't act on pressure. ✅
- D) Borrow money to buy more.

**Explanation:** urgency, huge promises and anonymous tips are classic warning signs.

### Q4. If you choose to speculate, which rule is the most important?
- A) Use your emergency fund.
- B) Use borrowed money to increase gains.
- C) Only use a small amount you can afford to lose completely. ✅
- D) Put all your savings in to maximize the chance of winning.

**Explanation:** speculation can go to zero, so it should never threaten important goals.

### Q5. What is FOMO?
- A) A type of bond
- B) The fear of missing out, which pushes people to buy because others are buying ✅
- C) A fund strategy
- D) A tax rule

**Explanation:** FOMO is an emotion, not a reason to invest.

### Q6 (reflection). Describe a situation (real or imagined) where hype could push someone into a bad decision. What should they have checked first?

**What a good answer mentions:** the pressure or emotion involved, and checks such as fit with their goal, how much they could lose, where the value comes from, and who is promoting it.
