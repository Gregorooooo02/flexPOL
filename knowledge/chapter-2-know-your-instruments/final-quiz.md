# Chapter 2 — Final Quiz: Know Your Instruments

**Covers:** 2.1 Cash and savings accounts · 2.2 Deposits and bonds · 2.3 Stocks · 2.4 Funds and ETFs · 2.5 Costs and fees · 2.6 High-risk and speculative assets · 2.7 Comparing instruments
**Questions:** 10 · **Suggested pass mark:** 7/10

> All numbers are fictional and simplified.

---

### Q1. What is the hidden risk of keeping long-term savings in cash?
- A) Cash pays very high fees.
- B) The money can't be withdrawn.
- C) Inflation can slowly reduce what the money can buy. ✅
- D) The balance swings up and down a lot.

**Explanation:** cash is stable and liquid, but over decades inflation eats its purchasing power. *(2.1)*

### Q2. When you buy a bond, what are you doing?
- A) Lending money to a government or company in exchange for interest ✅
- B) Buying a basket of stocks
- C) Opening a savings account
- D) Buying part of a company

**Explanation:** bond holders are lenders who receive interest and get the original amount back at maturity. *(2.2)*

### Q3. Two bonds have the same term. Bond X pays 3% and is issued by a stable government. Bond Y pays 9% and is issued by a struggling company. Why does Bond Y pay more?
- A) The company is more generous.
- B) Bond Y has a shorter term.
- C) Governments aren't allowed to pay more than 3%.
- D) There's a higher risk the company won't pay the money back. ✅

**Explanation:** higher credit risk has to be compensated with higher interest. *(2.2)*

### Q4. What are the two main ways a stock can make money for its owner?
- A) Coupons and deposits
- B) Price growth and dividends ✅
- C) Fees and penalties
- D) Interest and maturity

**Explanation:** shareholders can gain when the price rises and when the company shares profits. *(2.3)*

### Q5. Which is the easiest way for a beginner with a small amount of money to own hundreds of companies?
- A) Buy shares in one large company.
- B) Keep the money in a savings account.
- C) Buy a single bond.
- D) Buy a broad index fund or ETF. ✅

**Explanation:** one fund purchase gives a slice of everything the fund holds. *(2.4)*

### Q6. What does an index fund try to do?
- A) Match the return of a market by copying an index ✅
- B) Guarantee a fixed interest rate
- C) Invest only in cash
- D) Beat the market by picking the best stocks

**Explanation:** index funds follow a list of companies instead of trying to beat it, which keeps costs low. *(2.4)*

### Q7. 10,000 coins are invested for 30 years at a fictional 6% a year. Fund A charges 0% in fees, Fund B charges 1%. Roughly how much less does Fund B end with?
- A) About 140 coins
- B) About 1,400 coins
- C) About 14,000 coins ✅
- D) Exactly the same amount

**Explanation:** roughly 57,400 vs. 43,200 coins. A 1% yearly fee compounds against you for 30 years. *(2.5)*

### Q8. Which of these is a typical warning sign of a hype-driven investment?
- A) A long track record and broad diversification
- B) A promise of guaranteed high returns and pressure to "buy now" ✅
- C) Low, transparent fees
- D) A clear explanation of how the asset produces value

**Explanation:** guaranteed high returns and urgency are classic signs of speculation or a scheme. *(2.6)*

### Q9. Why can leveraged products be especially dangerous?
- A) They multiply losses as well as gains, and you can lose more than you put in. ✅
- B) They have no risk.
- C) They pay no returns.
- D) They can only be held for one day.

**Explanation:** borrowed money makes every price move bigger, in both directions. *(2.6)*

### Q10. Match the goals to the best-fitting instruments: an emergency fund, a home deposit needed in exactly 3 years, retirement in 30 years.
- A) Broad index fund, cash, 3-year term deposit
- B) Speculative asset, cash, 3-year term deposit
- C) 3-year term deposit, single stock, cash
- D) Cash, 3-year term deposit, broad index fund ✅

**Explanation:** each goal's horizon and liquidity needs point to a different tool. There is no single "best" instrument. *(2.7)*
