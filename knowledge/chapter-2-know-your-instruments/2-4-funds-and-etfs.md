# 2.4 Funds and ETFs

**Chapter:** 2 — Know Your Instruments
**Sandbox instrument:** Diversified market basket
**Goal of this subchapter:** the learner understands how funds bundle many investments, and the difference between index and actively managed funds.

---

## Lesson

### What a fund is

A **fund** pools money from many investors and spreads it across many investments, such as dozens or hundreds of stocks, bonds or both. When you buy a piece of the fund, you indirectly own a small slice of everything in it.

It's like buying a fruit basket instead of a single apple.

### ETFs

An **ETF** (*exchange-traded fund*) is a fund that can be bought and sold on a stock exchange during the day, like a single stock. Many ETFs follow an **index**.

### What is an index?

An **index** is a list of companies that represents a market or a part of it, for example "the 500 largest companies in a country" or "companies from around the world". An **index fund** simply buys everything in that list.

### Index funds vs. actively managed funds

| | Index (passive) fund | Actively managed fund |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Copies an index | A manager picks investments, trying to beat the market |
| Cost | Usually low | Usually higher |
| Goal | Match the market's return | Beat the market's return |
| Reality | Gets about the market return, minus small fees | Many active funds fail to beat their index over the long term, especially after fees |

### Strengths of funds

- **Instant diversification**, even with a small amount of money
- **Less effort** than picking individual stocks
- Index funds are often **low-cost**

### Limits

- A fund still falls when its whole market falls.
- Fees vary a lot, so check them (see 2.5).
- A fund focused on one narrow theme (for example, only one industry) is less diversified.

### Key takeaways

- A fund bundles many investments into one.
- An ETF is a fund traded on an exchange.
- Index funds copy a market and are usually cheap.
- Funds give easy diversification, but don't remove market risk.

## Control questions

### Q1. What does a fund do?
- A) Lends your money to a single company
- B) Pools money from many investors and spreads it across many investments ✅
- C) Guarantees a fixed return
- D) Keeps your money in cash

**Explanation:** pooling lets small investors own a slice of many assets.

### Q2. What does an index fund do?
- A) A manager picks the best stocks.
- B) It copies a list of companies that represents a market. ✅
- C) It invests only in cash.
- D) It invests in a single company.

**Explanation:** index funds follow an index instead of trying to beat it.

### Q3. Why are index funds usually cheaper than actively managed funds?
- A) They are smaller.
- B) They don't need a team researching and picking investments. ✅
- C) They are subsidized by the government.
- D) They aren't; they're always more expensive.

**Explanation:** following a list costs much less than active research and trading.

### Q4. The whole market falls 15%. What happens to a broad index fund?
- A) It's protected and stays flat.
- B) It falls roughly in line with the market. ✅
- C) It rises 15%.
- D) It closes.

**Explanation:** an index fund mirrors its market, in both directions.

### Q5. Which gives the most diversification for a small amount of money?
- A) Buying one stock
- B) Buying a broad global index fund ✅
- C) Buying two stocks in the same industry
- D) A single bond from one company

**Explanation:** one purchase gives exposure to hundreds or thousands of companies.

### Q6 (reflection). Why might a beginner choose an index fund instead of picking individual stocks?

**What a good answer mentions:** diversification, lower costs, less effort, and that beating the market consistently is hard even for professionals.
